# Trader with horses — Mahanabami Dibba relief

Row: `m1-p01-r03` · Type: `sculpture_relief`

## Subject & Composition

A standing figure occupies the centre between two inward-facing horses. The horses’ heads turn toward the figure, making the handler the point where the two halves of the panel meet.

## Pose & Gesture

The figure bends both arms outward toward the horses. A long garment falls toward the lower edge, and a pointed head covering rises above the face. The figures share a shallow horizontal ground.

## Material & Technique

The scene is carved in low relief on grainy stone. Cut outlines separate the horses’ heads, necks and legs from the background; the bodies project from the flat surrounding surface.

## Course Identification

Course reading: The index identifies the panel as a trader with horses from the Mahanabami Dibba at Hampi. Its return on slide 16 places the horse trader beside steel and textiles under ‘Trade connections’; slide 25 returns it to the module’s cultural/trade recap.

## Slide Context

Course reading: On the cover, the relief at right accompanies the floral dish at upper left (m1-p01-r01) and floral fabric at lower left (m1-p01-r02). The ceramic and textile share floral design; the stone relief contributes the trader, horses and movement of goods. Their complementary roles turn the three-medium juxtaposition into an argument about trade/contact and the circulation of artisanal knowledge. The Paigah Tombs and kalamkari hanging on slides 2–3 carry this argument into architecture and named textile practice.
