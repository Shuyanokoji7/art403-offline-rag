# Floral fabric detail — course cover

Row: `m1-p01-r02` · Type: `textile`

## Field & Border Organization

Two decorated panels flank a pale vertical strip. Red outer edges enclose cream floral borders, dark green upper fields and red lower fields. The borders follow the panels’ curved and angled edges.

## Colour & Motifs

Red flowers and long-tailed birds occupy the green fields. Gold-coloured linework outlines curling stems, leaves, petals and wings. Small red flowers repeat along the cream borders; blue flowers appear in the lower red field.

## Composition

The pale centre separates the paired patterned panels. Dense small-scale stems and flowers contrast with the dish’s larger, widely spaced floral forms above.

## Slide Context

Course reading: The lower-left textile is paired with the floral ceramic dish above (m1-p01-r01) and the stone trader-and-horses relief at right (m1-p01-r03), under the oceanic-networks heading. The recurring flowers connect design across ceramic and textile; the trader supplies the commercial and human relationship within which such cross-media connections are studied. The argument is about circulating motifs, goods and craft knowledge. The Paigah Tombs on slide 2 extend it into architectural ornament, and the kalamkari hanging on slide 3 makes material, technique and human interaction explicit.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The floral dish and fabric demonstrate that the same manufacturing technique was used on ceramic and cloth.”

**Why it is wrong:** The comparison concerns a design vocabulary across different supports. The dish arranges flowers concentrically; the fabric fits flowers and birds into panels and borders. Shared motifs do not make their manufacturing operations identical.
