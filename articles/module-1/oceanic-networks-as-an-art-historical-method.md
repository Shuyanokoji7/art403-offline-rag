# Oceanic networks as an art-historical method

## Argument

Course reading: The opening slides ask that artworks be studied through materials, techniques, themes and human interaction. Movement of goods is joined to movement of people and artisanal knowledge.

## Evidence & Artworks

Course reading: Paigah Tombs, the Janamaz/Hanging and the eight-module roadmap introduce the range from Hampi to Japonisme and contemporary exhibitions.

## Course Throughline

Course reading: Connected histories explain why regional objects can embody multiple contacts without losing their local uses.

## Slide Context

### Module 1, slide 1

**Heading context:** Ebb and Flow: Art and Globalism of Oceanic Networks

Course reading: The cover pairs a floral ceramic dish and floral fabric with a stone trader-and-horses relief under Ebb and Flow: Art and Globalism of Oceanic Networks. Repeated flowers connect ceramic and textile; the relief contributes the people and traded animals. This cross-media grouping argues for the circulation of goods, designs and artisanal knowledge through contact. The Paigah Tombs and Coromandel kalamkari hanging on slides 2–3 extend that argument into architectural ornament, textile making and human interaction.
