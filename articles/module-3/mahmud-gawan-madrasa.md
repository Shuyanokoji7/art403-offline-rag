# Mahmud Gawan Madrasa

## Building Type & Function

Course reading: Madrasa, a place of learning, identified by the caption.

## Structural System

Arcuate openings and monumental arched portal forms are visible.

## Elevation & Massing

The surviving multi-storey façade has repeated pointed openings, blue tile bands and a tall cylindrical corner minaret. The courtyard view reveals a large central arched recess opposite the viewer.

## Ornament Vocabulary

Tile-like blue and contrasting geometric bands articulate vertical towers and horizontal façade zones.

## Paired Comparison

Course reading: Slide 4 explicitly connects Bidar and Timurid Central Asia through the paired madrasa views.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The resemblance to Timurid madrasas makes Mahmud Gawan Madrasa a Central Asian building rather than a Deccani one.”

**Why it is wrong:** Mahmud Gawan Madrasa stands at Bidar. The Central Asian comparison addresses related architectural vocabulary adopted in the Deccan, not the building’s geographical relocation.
