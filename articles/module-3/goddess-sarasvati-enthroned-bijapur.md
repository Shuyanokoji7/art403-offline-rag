# Goddess Sarasvati enthroned — Bijapur

## Composition

The seated goddess occupies a large golden throne under a canopy. Winged attendants flank its upper sides. A small panel of ruler and elephant crowns the composition.

## Perspective Convention

Angled throne edges and stacked compartments combine several spatial conventions rather than one continuous room.

## Figural Style

Jewellery, looping pale drapery and a profile face distinguish the central figure. A standing figure at lower right approaches the throne.

## Text–Image Relationship

Small script appears on the throne steps and beneath the top panel.

## Motifs & Hidden Details

A peacock stretches across the bottom. Blue-and-white vases with flowers stand beside the throne. Small birds sit near the backrest. Two white birds flank a tiny figure in the lowermost medallion.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The Sarasvati painting proves that Ibrahim Adil Shah II converted to Hinduism.”

**Why it is wrong:** The pairing connects Sarasvati’s devotional and musical associations with Bijapuri court culture. It establishes that pictorial relationship, not a conversion by the patron.
