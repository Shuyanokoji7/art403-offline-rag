# French — Issen Yoshikazu

## Process

Course reading: Polychrome print, captioned ōban size.

A Japanese printmaking context represents Europeans and their camera.

## Edition & Circulation

Course reading: The caption gives 1861 and a Japanese collection, situating representation within encounter with foreign residents and technologies.

## Composition & Motifs

A man in dark coat and pale trousers bends beneath a cloth at a tripod camera on the right. A bonneted woman stands behind. Small bottles and equipment sit on a table at lower left. Script runs down the upper and lower right.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: European photographic practice becomes the subject of a Japanese printed image.

## Slide Context

### Module 7, slide 14

**Heading context:** Photography and photographers in Japan

Course reading: The Tokyo street photograph and Yoshikazu’s print of French people using a camera reverse the direction of looking. Japan appears as an urban photographic subject while Europeans become the subject of a Japanese print. Orientalism/Occidentalism is therefore discussed through representation and agency, not simply the nationality of an image’s subject.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Yoshikazu’s French is a photograph produced by the camera represented within it.”

**Why it is wrong:** This is a print depicting photography, not a photograph taken by the man it represents.
