# Water Lilies and Japanese Bridge — Monet

## Composition

A pale curved bridge crosses the middle of dense greenery. Its reflection and the lily-covered pond fill the lower half. Hanging foliage descends from above.

## Palette & Pigment

Green and blue dominate, punctuated by pale bridge rails and pink-white blossoms.

## Line & Hand

Short broken strokes intermingle foliage, water and reflected structure, contrasting with Hokusai’s more sharply bounded shapes.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: A Japanese-inspired garden bridge is represented in Monet’s painting. The process remains painting rather than Japanese relief printing.

## Slide Context

### Module 7, slide 11

**Heading context:** Van Gogh, the search of sunlight and “Japan” in Arles, France, 1888 (continued from slide 6)

Course reading: Hokusai’s Mannen Bridge and Monet’s Japanese bridge are paired through the bridge’s graphic curve. The populated urban crossing and garden pond have different scales, settings and media. The comparison makes a formal relation explicit without establishing that Monet copied this exact impression.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Monet’s bridge serves the same urban transport role as Hokusai’s populated Mannen Bridge.”

**Why it is wrong:** Monet’s bridge has a different scale, setting and function from the populated urban bridge in Hokusai’s print.
- **Parallel — [Under Mannen Bridge at Fukagawa](<under-mannen-bridge-at-fukagawa.md>):** Course reading: Both compositions place a curved bridge over water: Hokusai uses a populated urban setting, while Monet surrounds the bridge with garden planting.
