# Under the Wave off Kanagawa — The Great Wave

## Process

Course reading: Japanese colour woodblock print: relief printing uses aligned impressions from carved blocks.

## Edition & Circulation

Course reading: The module treats Japanese prints as portable objects collected and reinterpreted in Europe.

## Composition & Motifs

The enormous wave curls from upper left over three narrow boats with bent rowers. White foam branches like claws, while tiny snow-topped Fuji sits near the lower centre of the distant horizon. A title cartouche and signature occupy upper left.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: Japanese landscape and labour are represented through Japanese printmaking. Later European responses may borrow composition while using other media.

## Slide Context

### Module 7, slide 1

**Heading context:** Module 7: Wave of Japonisme

Course reading: The Great Wave, the introductory ukiyo-e image and Lake Chuzenji establish a Japanese print vocabulary of framing, flat colour, landscape and scale. They prepare the following Van Gogh–Eisen comparison. The Japanese examples remain distinct works rather than interchangeable illustrations of one national style.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The Great Wave is a European oil painting because it influenced European painters.”

**Why it is wrong:** The Great Wave is a Japanese woodblock print. Its later influence on European painting concerns reception, not a change in the source object’s medium.
