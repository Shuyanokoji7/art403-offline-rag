# The Letter — Mary Cassatt

## External Checks

- **Verification:** The NGA records the final colour print as softground etching, drypoint and aquatint. The Met’s separate first-state record is drypoint; do not transfer that state-specific label to the finished colour impression. [Mary Cassatt, The Letter — National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/168826-letter) [Mary Cassatt, The Letter, first state — The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/352204)

## Process

Course reading: The lecture pairs Cassatt’s colour print with a Japanese design to compare composition. The article’s External Checks identifies softground etching, drypoint and aquatint as the processes of the finished print.

## Edition & Circulation

Course reading: The work belongs to Cassatt’s engagement with Japanese prints through a different printmaking practice.

## Composition & Motifs

A woman in a blue patterned garment bends toward a white envelope at her mouth. A sharply angled writing surface carries another sheet. Pale floral wallpaper fills the background.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: A European domestic subject is rendered by an American artist using intaglio methods informed by Japanese print aesthetics.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Cassatt’s The Letter is a woodblock directly derived from Mizuno’s later After the Bath.”

**Why it is wrong:** The finished Letter uses intaglio processes, including aquatint, rather than woodblock. Its 1890–91 date also precedes the 1893 Mizuno print, making that pairing parallel rather than a direct source relationship.
- **Parallel — [After the Bath — Woman of the Kansei Era](<after-the-bath-woman-of-the-kansei-era.md>):** Course reading: The slide illustrates formal affinities; chronology excludes direct derivation from this later print.
