# Kochi-Muziris Biennale and reclaiming global ties

## Argument

Course reading: The lecture connects a contemporary biennale and its local public campaign to Kochi/Muziris’s longer trade histories. Artworks bring Biblical, industrial and colonial connections into present exhibition space.

## Evidence & Artworks

Course reading: Street posters, Raghavan’s container ship, Tallur’s tile environment and Quintessential address different scales of connection.

## Course Throughline

Course reading: Regional identity is represented through outward connections and contested histories, not through isolation.

### Module 8, slide 19

**Heading context:** Archaeological Excavation in Elephanta and Colonial Legacy Reimagined

Course reading: The framed elephant image is placed in relation to Quintessential’s screen, sculptural objects and fragmented forms. Under the Elephanta and colonial-legacy heading, archival representation and contemporary installation rework historical authority. Precise spatial relationships supply the curatorial argument, not the mere fact that several objects share a room.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Reclaiming global ties proves that Kochi’s ancient trade routes continued unchanged into the present.”

**Why it is wrong:** Reclaiming global ties is a curatorial-historical argument, not proof that every ancient maritime connection remained uninterrupted.
