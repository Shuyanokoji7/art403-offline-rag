# Belitung cargo — bowls arranged as waves around a dhow

## Physical Arrangement

Dozens of bowls tilt upward on slender transparent rods at graduated heights. The rods build a wave-like crest at right and lower troughs in front. A model sailing dhow sits centrally behind the foreground bowls, with visitors visible beyond the enclosure. The close view shows repeated but varied brown painted bowl designs.

## What the Arrangement Communicates

Course reading: Repetition makes cargo quantity and serial production visible. The undulating heights and model ship restore an image of maritime transport. This is a curatorial reconstruction, not the recovered archaeological arrangement.

## Objects & Origins

Course reading: The lecture describes Chinese Tang-period cargo from a merchant dhow wrecked near Belitung around 830, exhibited in Singapore. The model is interpretive display furniture. It is not the excavated hull.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The Belitung display places identical bowls in neutral storage rows, with no representation of sea travel.”

**Why it is wrong:** The bowls rise in undulating rows around a dhow, turning repeated cargo into a wave-like environment. The arrangement links production scale and sea transport through its specific form.
