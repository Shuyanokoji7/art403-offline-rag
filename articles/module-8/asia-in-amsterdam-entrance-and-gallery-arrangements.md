# Asia in Amsterdam — entrance and gallery arrangements

## Physical Arrangement

At the entrance, large exhibition lettering fills a deep blue wall at right. Two decorated cylindrical objects occupy stepped blue niches along the route. An enlarged harbour scene covers the rear-left wall, with a floral hanging visible beyond it. In the smaller gallery view, a narrative tapestry spans the left wall, two tall glass cases stand on the floor, and framed pictures run along the right wall toward a doorway. In the long gallery, cabinets and textiles line the left, still-life paintings line the right, and central cases, paired tall vessels and a rear screen establish an axis under the skylight. The nearest right-hand still life visibly includes a blue-and-white ewer.

## What the Arrangement Communicates

Course reading: Placing functional luxury objects beside pictures of luxury goods lets the viewer compare circulating material things with their painted representations. Sightlines between rooms extend that comparison beyond isolated cases. The quoted review asks how Asian imports shaped Dutch identity. That argument is distinct from the physical description.

## Objects & Origins

Course reading: Asia in Amsterdam, Rijksmuseum, 2015–16, is the captioned exhibition. The review mentions Bouke de Vries’s concluding works.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Asia in Amsterdam displays goods and paintings independently, without using their placement to connect imports and consumption.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: The gallery places imported goods near paintings of luxury consumption, using cases, wall hangings and sightlines to make viewers compare them. Their physical relation supplies the trade argument.
