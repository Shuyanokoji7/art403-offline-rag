# Kali and Tara — Calcutta Art Studio

## External Checks

- **Verification:** The Met identifies this diptych as an 1885–90 chromolithograph by Calcutta Art Studio, object 2018.292. This verifies the colour-print process. [Diptych of Kali / Tara, wrathful protectors — The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/786756)

## Process

Course reading: The lecture attributes the colour print to Calcutta Art Studio. The article’s External Checks documents the sheet as a chromolithograph.

## Edition & Circulation

Course reading: Names in multiple scripts and studio/copyright lines appear on the bottom margin, making the sheet a published devotional commodity.

## Composition & Motifs

The sheet is split vertically into two goddess images. Kali at left has four arms, loose dark hair, a garland of heads, a blade and severed head, with reclining bodies below. Tara at right carries a lotus, blade and bowl-like object. A skull garland, skull crown, tiger-skin garment and snakes distinguish her. Flames rise on both sides of Tara’s feet, with a pale reclining body beneath. A loose skull and bones appear along the lower left.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: Bengali religious iconography is reproduced through a modern colour-print enterprise.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Kali and Tara are identical repetitions of one goddess made by hand-colouring a monochrome sheet.”

**Why it is wrong:** Kali and Tara are not interchangeable labels for one repeated figure; compare attributes, garlands, ground and body treatment.
