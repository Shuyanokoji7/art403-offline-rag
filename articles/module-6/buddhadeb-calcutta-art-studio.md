# Buddhadeb — Calcutta Art Studio

## Process

Course reading: Chromolithograph according to slide 20: colour lithographic printing, rather than a hand-painted Kalighat sheet.

## Edition & Circulation

Course reading: A named indigenous studio places a Buddhist subject within the expanding popular print market.

## Composition & Motifs

The frontal seated Buddha raises his right palm and rests the other hand at his lap. A lotus pedestal, decorated pillars and red curtain frame him. Small kneeling figures occupy lower architectural niches. Distant spires appear at right.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: South Asian religious subject matter is produced with European-developed lithographic technology in a Calcutta studio.

## Slide Context

### Module 6, slide 1

**Heading context:** Module 6: European Print and Indian Bazaar

Course reading: The Calcutta Art Studio Buddha and the Agra print introduce two uses of reproducible imagery: devotional circulation and a published architectural view. The Agra credits separate sketch, drawing and engraving. European print technology and South Asian subjects therefore require separate statements about medium, audience and labour.
