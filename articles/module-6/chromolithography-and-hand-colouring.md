# Chromolithography and hand-colouring

## Definition

Course reading: Chromolithography produces colour through successive lithographic printings, requiring alignment. Hand-colouring adds colour manually to an existing impression. A work may combine them.

## Nearest Confusable Terms

Course reading: A colour woodcut is not a chromolithograph. A hand-tinted chromolithograph is not a contradiction. Misregistration concerns alignment of printed components, not every irregular edge of applied colour.

## Applied To

Course reading: Buddhadeb and Hawk are captioned chromolithographs. Vijaya is hand-tinted chromolithography. Scene in Bombay uses etching, aquatint and hand-colouring.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Any coloured print is a chromolithograph, including one coloured by hand after printing.”

**Why it is wrong:** Chromolithography prints colour through lithographic operations. Hand-colouring adds colour after an impression is printed; the two processes can also be combined in one object.
