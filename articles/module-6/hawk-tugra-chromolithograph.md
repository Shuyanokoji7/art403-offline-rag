# Hawk — Tugra chromolithograph

## Process

Course reading: Chromolithograph, as captioned.

## Edition & Circulation

Course reading: The captions locate these prints in Delhi/Lahore or Delhi and identify V&A examples.

## Composition & Motifs

Bold calligraphy forms a large hawk bending over a smaller bird. Pale blue sky and ochre ground surround the black graphic silhouette.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: Zoomorphic calligraphic imagery is reproduced with lithography. The depicted animal and written text are inseparable parts of the design.

## Slide Context

### Module 6, slide 17

**Heading context:** Indigenous Painting practices and Printmaking (continued from slide 13)

Course reading: The lithographed book cover, tiger-shaped script and hawk-shaped script compare writing as readable content and constructed image. Lithography and colour lithography provide different printed realizations. The grouping connects text, animal outline and circulation rather than treating the animal forms as natural-history studies.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The hawk’s chromolithographic process and the tiger’s lithographic label identify the same colour-printing specification.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: The hawk is labelled chromolithograph, specifying colour lithographic production; the tiger has the broader lithograph label. Neither label supplies a counted number of matrices.
