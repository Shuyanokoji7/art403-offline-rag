# Shah Jahan on a Terrace Holding a Portrait Pendant

## External Checks

- **Verification:** The Met records Chitarman’s recto as 1627–28, folio from the Shah Jahan Album, accession 55.121.10.24. The catalogue identifies European angel imagery and the representation of several courtly crafts. Recto painting and album assembly are distinct. [Chitarman, Shah Jahan holding a portrait pendant — The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/451270)

## Composition

A standing ruler on a small yellow platform occupies the centre of a terrace, below a vast radiant halo. Receding water and trees lie behind a low white screen.

## Perspective Convention

The platform and patterned carpet recede obliquely, while the profile body retains a formal frontal-plane presentation.

## Figural Style

A pink floral robe, plumed turban, necklaces, sash and sword frame the small raised pendant containing the ruler’s own portrait.

## Text–Image Relationship

Calligraphy frames the inner image, then coloured floral and broad gold-coloured margins surround it.

## Motifs & Hidden Details

Winged figures appear at the upper corners and top centre. Tiny boats dot the water. White pierced railing panels stand behind the platform.

## Subject Matter vs Technique Origin

Course reading: Mughal imperial self-representation incorporates radiating halo, angels and spatial devices discussed as European elements in the lecture.
