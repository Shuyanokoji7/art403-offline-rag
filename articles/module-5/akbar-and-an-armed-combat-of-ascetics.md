# Akbar and an armed combat of ascetics

## Composition

A dense knot of combatants fills the lower two-thirds. The mounted ruler and court attendants occupy the calmer upper band. A distant settlement sits beyond trees at the top.

## Perspective Convention

The high viewpoint exposes many overlapping bodies, weapons and shields. Distance is organized by vertical stacking and diminishing figures.

## Line & Hand

Fine contour separates many entwined limbs.

Course reading: The caption credits Basawan. The lecture explains a collaborative Persianate–Indian atelier rather than a culturally isolated hand.

## Figural Style

Bare or lightly clothed ascetics wield curved blades, staffs and circular shields, contrasting with the elaborate dress of the ruler’s party.

## Motifs & Hidden Details

Two elephants and a red parasol stand at upper right. A decorated standard rises near the rider. Round weapon-like rings punctuate the melee.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Akbar’s combat-of-ascetics scene uses the same quiet, contained figure grouping as Virgin of the Rocks.”

**Why it is wrong:** A scene with ascetics is not necessarily a devotional image; here the subject is armed conflict viewed within a court narrative.
