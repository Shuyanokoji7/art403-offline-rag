# Globe in the Mughal Court

## Argument

Course reading: European prints, books and cartographic forms entered Mughal courtly image-making and were selectively adapted. The module asks how foreign visual resources became tools for local political and religious expression.

## Evidence & Artworks

Course reading: Christian-themed paintings, the Gulshan composite page, artists’ studies, and Jahangir’s two globe allegories show different mechanisms of transfer.

## Course Throughline

Course reading: Trade and travel move images and skills as well as goods. Indian-centred cartography and transformed religious imagery make the receiving court an active interpreter.

### Module 5, slide 18

**Heading context:** Establishing Lineage and Connections through Art Styles

Course reading: The Gulshan sheet assembles a Luna engraving with Persian drawings of a seated man, a group of men and a lion. The whole and its components must be read together: collecting, cutting, mounting and framing create a new relationship among previously separate images. One page therefore need not have one maker, date or original medium.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Imported European imagery was copied passively by the Mughal court without adaptation.”

**Why it is wrong:** Circulation does not imply passive copying or uniform adoption of European naturalism.
