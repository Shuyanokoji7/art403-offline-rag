# Popular prints and wider circulation

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Print processes enabled multiple impressions and wider image circulation. The Calcutta Kali/Tara diptych is a documented colour-print case. The lecture includes devotional, commercial and social subjects.

## Attribution Note

Not established: edition or impression state.

## Slide Context

**M6:1**

Course reading: The Calcutta Art Studio Buddha and the Agra print introduce two uses of reproducible imagery: devotional circulation and a published architectural view. The Agra credits separate sketch, drawing and engraving. European print technology and South Asian subjects therefore require separate statements about medium, audience and labour.

**M6:3**

Course reading: The unfinished Pedana woodblocks and finished Polavaram cotton connect a carved matrix to textile printing. This precedes the European Saint Sebastian block-and-impression pair. The sequence compares a printing principle across supports and uses, while keeping the specific practices distinct.

**M6:7**

Course reading: The Sicri Gully and Salsette temple prints are followed by the Antiquities of India title page. Views, maker credits and antiquarian publication framing connect Indian sites with a British audience. Pictorial composition and the date of a published plate remain separate from the date of a visit or monument.

**M6:9, M6:12**

Course reading: Solvyns’s potters and bazaar, the Kanaree cave view and Roxburgh’s botanical plate form a sequence of people, places and plants under colonial documentation. Each image organizes knowledge differently. The grouping asks who observes, draws, commissions and publishes, as well as what is represented.

**M6:13, M6:14**

Course reading: The broom scene and cat-with-lobster Kalighat painting are followed by the cat motif in a Battala print. Social commentary and a recognizable subject pass between painting and print. The repeated cat is therefore a comparison of pictorial treatment and circulation, not evidence that the two objects share one manufacturing process.

**M6:15, M6:16**

Course reading: Shiva playing a veena, the two Mahant–Elokeshi scenes and Durga with her family show the range of indigenous popular imagery. Religious subjects and a public social narrative share the market without becoming one story. Distinguish the separate encounter and violence scenes when reading the paired scandal images.

**M6:17**

Course reading: The lithographed book cover, tiger-shaped script and hawk-shaped script compare writing as readable content and constructed image. Lithography and colour lithography provide different printed realizations. The grouping connects text, animal outline and circulation rather than treating the animal forms as natural-history studies.

**M6:18**

Course reading: The Bombay scene and Annoda Prosad Bagchi’s Vijaya pair different makers and subjects within the print economy. They follow the book and calligraphic prints and precede studio devotional sheets. The comparison expands the field of print production beyond a single colonial publisher or one religious audience.

**M6:19, M6:20, M6:21**

Course reading: Kali/Tara, the Calcutta Art Studio Buddha and the Pune Mahavir print form a sequence of studio devotional images. Different religious subjects use reproducible colour imagery and commercial publication. The significant distinction is between the regional subject, the technology and the agency of the producing studio.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A popular devotional subject guarantees anonymous authorship and an identical audience.”

**Why it is wrong:** Popular subject matter does not mean anonymous authorship, identical editions or one uniform audience.
