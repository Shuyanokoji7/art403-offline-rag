# Aquatint: printed tone

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Aquatint produces tonal areas within an intaglio process. It appears in the documented techniques of Cassatt’s The Letter and the Daniells’ title sheet.

## Slide Context

**M6:18**

Course reading: The Bombay scene and Annoda Prosad Bagchi’s Vijaya pair different makers and subjects within the print economy. They follow the book and calligraphic prints and precede studio devotional sheets. The comparison expands the field of print production beyond a single colonial publisher or one religious audience.

**M7:10**

Course reading: La Japonaise, Cassatt’s The Letter and Mizuno’s After the Bath compare costume performance, domestic pictorial design and a Japanese print. The three illustrate different forms of Japanese reception. Cassatt’s intaglio medium remains distinct from woodblock, and Mizuno’s later print cannot be her direct source.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A wash-like appearance makes Cassatt’s colour print a unique watercolour.”

**Why it is wrong:** Cassatt’s finished Letter is a colour print made with softground etching, drypoint and aquatint. Aquatint supplies printed tonal areas; their wash-like appearance does not turn the sheet into a unique watercolour.
