# Chahar bagh: quadripartite ordering

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Persian garden tradition combines organized divisions with water management and associations of paradise. The course considers related ordering at garden and urban scales. A grid-like city plan is a comparison to that model, not proof that every quadrant remained a planted garden.

## Slide Context

**M1:24**

Course reading: Lotus Mahal and Charminar are brought together through stucco and cultural connections across the Deccan. Ornament supplies the bridge between distinct buildings and urban settings. This comparison develops the previous slide’s account of architectural combination rather than declaring the two monuments identical in structure or function.

**M3:5**

Course reading: The well, basin and Sola Khamba Masjid share the Bidar landscape slide. Water infrastructure and a religious building are placed within the discussion of Persianate architecture and garden planning. Their grouping concerns a spatial environment with different functions, rather than three interchangeable building types.

**M3:21**

Course reading: The Hyderabad map and street view toward Charminar pair planned urban order with a view through monumental space. Under City of Gardens, the lecture applies chahar-bagh ordering at city scale. The older Lotus Mahal comparison supplies another architectural connection, while the map has its own later date.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Fourfold urban ordering proves that four intact planted gardens occupied the mapped city.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: Fourfold ordering, garden symbolism and a historically documented garden are distinct claims.
