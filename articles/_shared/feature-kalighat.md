# Kalighat painting and the urban market

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Kalighat painters made images for a growing urban and pilgrimage market, often using bold contours and comparatively open grounds. Subjects included deities and social commentary. The economic setting and efficient handling should be considered alongside the finished image’s aesthetic decisions.

## Slide Context

**M6:13**

Course reading: The broom scene and cat-with-lobster Kalighat painting are followed by the cat motif in a Battala print. Social commentary and a recognizable subject pass between painting and print. The repeated cat is therefore a comparison of pictorial treatment and circulation, not evidence that the two objects share one manufacturing process.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A Kalighat image is automatically a lithograph because it was made for a popular market.”

**Why it is wrong:** Kalighat painting is not automatically a lithographic print; a familiar popular subject does not identify the process.
