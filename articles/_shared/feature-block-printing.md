# Block printing on cloth

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: A prepared block repeats a design on the textile surface. It can work alongside other painting and dye stages. Repetition suggests a useful question about manufacture. Does not, by itself, prove the use of a block.

## Slide Context

**M2:5**

Course reading: The Srikalahasti drawing demonstration and Polavaram block-printing demonstration are paired across adjacent slides, with a finished Coromandel hanging beside the latter. They separate making with a pen, repeating with a block and the resulting coloured cloth. The comparison gives craft labour a place alongside design and trade.

**M2:15**

Course reading: The Coromandel dodot moves the discussion from motif comparison to use in Southeast Asia. Its pairing in the sequence with European chintz garments on slide 16 shows a production region serving different dress forms and markets. Manufacture and destination are separate roles in the argument.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A repeated flower proves that the textile was made exclusively by block printing.”

**Why it is wrong:** A repeated flower is a design observation; a block-printed technique requires process evidence.
