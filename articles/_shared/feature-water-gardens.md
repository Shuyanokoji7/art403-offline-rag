# Water, gardens and stepped tanks

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Water can structure both use and visual order: channels divide gardens, while stepped tanks expose changing levels through repeated terraces. UNESCO documents hydraulic systems at Hampi and water management in Persian gardens.

## Slide Context

**M1:5, M1:6**

Course reading: The empire and city maps establish several scales for reading the Mahanabami Dibba: territorial power, the Hampi landscape and individual monuments. The following temple hall and sculpture comparison turn this geographical frame into an investigation of structure, carving and historical difference.

**M1:12**

Course reading: The stepped tank is paired with the Rama Temple relief of festival and warfare. The juxtaposition asks how water architecture, movement and ritual imagery might relate to Narasimha and Vasantotsava. This is the lecture’s posed connection, while the preceding royal platform supplies the political and ceremonial setting.

**M2:3**

Course reading: The Coromandel map, route diagram and riverside temple landscape connect textile production to places, movement and built environments. This sequence leads from the opening palampore to named making practices. A historical map and a modern route diagram contribute different kinds of geographical evidence.

**M3:5**

Course reading: The well, basin and Sola Khamba Masjid share the Bidar landscape slide. Water infrastructure and a religious building are placed within the discussion of Persianate architecture and garden planning. Their grouping concerns a spatial environment with different functions, rather than three interchangeable building types.

**M3:17**

Course reading: Gol Gumbaz’s exterior and structural diagram are followed by a view of another domed complex. The sequence compares the visual massing and setting of Deccani funerary architecture. Keep the uncaptioned complex separate from the named Gol Gumbaz and its documented internal arrangement.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A tank’s proximity to a temple establishes one exclusive ritual use.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: A water feature can have practical and ceremonial roles; a specific ritual requires evidence beyond proximity to a temple.
