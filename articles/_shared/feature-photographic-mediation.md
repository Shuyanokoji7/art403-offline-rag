# Photographs as mediated evidence

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Beato material documents staged or selected views and hand colouring involving additional makers. A photograph records a particular view while also reflecting posing, framing, market and production choices. A museum installation photograph likewise represents one viewpoint and moment.

## Slide Context

**M7:12, M7:13**

Course reading: The photographic portraits of Monet and Cassatt are followed by Beato’s staged performers. These photographs connect the artists and Japan to specific modes of representation. Sitter identification, photographic authorship, staging and hand-colouring are separate questions rather than one transparent documentary fact.

**M7:14**

Course reading: The Tokyo street photograph and Yoshikazu’s print of French people using a camera reverse the direction of looking. Japan appears as an urban photographic subject while Europeans become the subject of a Japanese print. Orientalism/Occidentalism is therefore discussed through representation and agency, not simply the nationality of an image’s subject.

**M8:11**

Course reading: Belitung bowls arranged as waves around a dhow are compared with underwater documentation and Kerkar’s encrusted cross. The museum uses repeated cargo to give production and shipping a spatial form; the underwater image records another stage of the objects’ lives. The cross introduces the ocean’s material participation in contemporary making.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A photograph’s optical appearance proves an unstaged and complete record.”

**Why it is wrong:** Photographic appearance is not proof of unstaged documentary neutrality or access to everything outside the frame.
