# Sfumato: softened transitions

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Sfumato describes softened transitions that avoid sharply bounded outlines, associated with Leonardo’s handling. In the lecture’s comparison, it should be distinguished from the presence of a dark background alone. The cited National Gallery page concerns the London Virgin of the Rocks, not an attribution of the slide’s version.

## Slide Context

**M5:8**

Course reading: Akbar’s combat-of-ascetics scene and Leonardo’s Virgin of the Rocks place Mughal atelier practice beside European Renaissance composition. Crowded action and the contained devotional grouping provide different organizations of figures and space. The sequence broadens artistic encounter beyond a claim of simple copying.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A dark background alone establishes sfumato.”

**Why it is wrong:** Sfumato concerns softened contours and gradual tonal transitions, illustrated around faces in the Leonardo comparison. A dark background describes a colour-value setting rather than that treatment of edges.
