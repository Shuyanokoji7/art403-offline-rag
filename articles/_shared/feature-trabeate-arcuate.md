# Trabeate and arcuate construction in the Deccan

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Trabeate construction spans between supports with horizontal members. Arcuate construction uses arches and associated vault or dome systems. Hampi buildings can combine architectural vocabularies. The corpus distinguishes a visible post-and-beam hall from an arch-shaped decorative outline whose actual load path is not exposed.

## Slide Context

**M1:7**

Course reading: The Krishna Temple hall follows the Hampi maps and precedes the Badami–Vijayanagara sculpture comparison. Its carved posts, horizontal beams and animal supports connect the scale of a building to the sculptural forms through which the next slide compares periods.

**M1:19**

Course reading: The Virupaksha procession mural is placed beside its temple gateway under Images of procession cross Media. Painted movement and monumental setting are read together, continuing the earlier relief and textile comparisons. The following royal bronzes and Lepakshi mural extend the question from processions to the representation of rulers.

**M1:21**

Course reading: Vijaya Vitthala friezes, animal-bearing columns and three warrior panels extend the enquiry into interregional imagery beyond the royal platform. Compare clothing, weapons, pose and composite animals within their architectural positions. Differences among the three warriors matter: they are separate carved figures, not duplicate views of one person.

**M1:23**

Course reading: The Lotus Mahal combines arches, posts, projecting eaves and tiered roof forms under an explicit heading about temple, palatial and Islamic architecture. The argument concerns the combination of structural and ornamental vocabularies within a courtly building. The next slide’s Charminar comparison follows those vocabularies across the Deccan.

**M1:24**

Course reading: Lotus Mahal and Charminar are brought together through stucco and cultural connections across the Deccan. Ornament supplies the bridge between distinct buildings and urban settings. This comparison develops the previous slide’s account of architectural combination rather than declaring the two monuments identical in structure or function.

**M2:14**

Course reading: A coverlet detail and the north doorway of Hayat Baksh’s tomb are paired under Pineapple: a fruit of the trade networks. The lecture follows a plant motif between textile and architecture to connect decoration with moving goods and designs. It extends the earlier comparisons without equating the objects’ functions.

**M3:3, M3:4**

Course reading: Mahmud Gawan Madrasa is compared with Ulugh Beg’s madrasa and the Registan under Timurid Central Asian culture in Deccan. Monumental entrances, towers and patterned surfaces supply the architectural comparison. The next Bidar landscape slide broadens the enquiry to water, gardens and the siting of buildings.

**M3:5**

Course reading: The well, basin and Sola Khamba Masjid share the Bidar landscape slide. Water infrastructure and a religious building are placed within the discussion of Persianate architecture and garden planning. Their grouping concerns a spatial environment with different functions, rather than three interchangeable building types.

**M3:10**

Course reading: The Kitab-e-Nauras page and Sangeet Mahal pair a literary-musical work with an architectural setting associated with Ibrahim Adil Shah II. They continue the Sarasvati and ruler comparison from slide 9 and lead into the tambur portrait. Cultural patronage is expressed across text, image, music and building.

**M3:16, M3:17**

Course reading: Gol Gumbaz’s exterior and structural diagram are followed by a view of another domed complex. The sequence compares the visual massing and setting of Deccani funerary architecture. Keep the uncaptioned complex separate from the named Gol Gumbaz and its documented internal arrangement.

**M3:21**

Course reading: The Hyderabad map and street view toward Charminar pair planned urban order with a view through monumental space. Under City of Gardens, the lecture applies chahar-bagh ordering at city scale. The older Lotus Mahal comparison supplies another architectural connection, while the map has its own later date.

**M5:9**

Course reading: Akbar’s Church is placed between Renaissance imagery and the Mughal Deposition. The building supplies a setting for the module’s discussion of religious communities and encounters. It links architecture to the circulation of Christian subjects without assigning every surviving architectural part to one original phase.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Trabeate and arcuate systems are exclusively owned by separate religions in every building.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: The contrast is a structural vocabulary used in the course, not an exclusive religious ownership rule for all buildings.
