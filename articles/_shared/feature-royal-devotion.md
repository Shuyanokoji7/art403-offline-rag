# Royal power and devotional imagery

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The course juxtaposes courtly authority with sacred subjects, including ceremonial architecture and devotional images produced in court settings. Hampi’s royal and sacred complexes and the Sarasvati material provide grounded examples. Political and religious dimensions can coexist without every religious image being a royal commission.

## Slide Context

**M1:20**

Course reading: The three Tirumala bronzes of Krishnadevaraya and his queens are grouped with the Lepakshi mural of kabai and kullayi dress. Courtly clothing and devotional posture connect royal identity to sacred representation across bronze and paint. The preceding procession and following temple carvings locate the ruler within a broader visual programme.

**M3:9**

Course reading: Ibrahim Adil Shah on Atash Khan is paired with enthroned Sarasvati under cultural amalgamation. Courtly self-presentation and a deity of learning and music share the discussion. The next Kitab-e-Nauras page and Sangeet Mahal translate this relationship into text, patronage and a musical setting.

**M3:19**

Course reading: The zoomorphic dagger hilt is followed by Sultan Ali Adil Shah I under a question about their relationship. A portable court object and a ruler’s portrait link animal ornament, costume and elite identity. The juxtaposition asks how object culture and represented kingship illuminate one another.

**M3:25**

Course reading: Farrukh Beg’s presumed self-portrait, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Sultan Ali Adil Shah I recap the module through travelling artists, marbled technique and courtly identity. Each supplies a different mechanism of connection. Their grouping makes movement of people, processes and imagery jointly significant.

**M5:16**

Course reading: The queried Dürer Crucifixion, Abu’l Hasan’s Saint John and Abdullah Khan’s hawking scene place European print study alongside Mughal portraiture and courtly activity. These examples belong to the lineage discussion while differing in subject and making process. A European religious figure supplies a visual resource, not the identity of every local patron.
