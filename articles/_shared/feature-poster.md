# Posters: publicity, image and placement

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The lecture shows entertainment advertisements and contemporary exhibition publicity, including repeated designs on Kochi walls. Lettering, image hierarchy, repeated display and physical placement are observable.

## Slide Context

**M7:9**

Course reading: Jane Avril and Divan Japonais place Japanese-inspired cropping and contour within Parisian entertainment publicity. Performer, spectator and instrument are organized differently in the two posters. Their subject and lithographic process remain distinct from Japanese woodblock manufacture.

**M8:16**

Course reading: The Fort Kochi opening poster and two It’s my Biennale portrait designs put an international exhibition into ordinary local streets. Repetition and the first-person slogan connect public ownership with place. The following installations show how the event’s regional identity is developed through particular works and historical connections.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The photographer who documents a poster necessarily designed and printed it.”

**Why it is wrong:** The poster’s designer, its printer and the photographer documenting it perform separate operations. A photograph records an already arranged title, image and lettering; photographic credit is not automatically design or printing credit.
