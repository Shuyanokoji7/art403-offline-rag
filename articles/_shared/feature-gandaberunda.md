# Gandaberunda: the two-headed bird

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Deccan exhibition catalogue identifies the two-headed bird in the tent-panel context. Museum comparisons document different receptions of this form, including Indian and European heraldic uses. Count heads, bodies and surrounding attributes before identifying a bird.

## Slide Context

**M2:6, M2:7**

Course reading: The five-panel tent and enlarged two-headed bird are compared with a Manga malai necklace and a Dutch-cemetery gravestone. The Gandaberunda form connects textile, jewellery and carved stone across different uses. The grouping demonstrates motif mobility; the ritual, military and commemorative functions must still be distinguished.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The two-headed bird has the same religious meaning on the Dutch grave and Deccani tent.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: A two-headed bird on a Dutch grave and one on a Deccani textile are comparable motifs, not automatically the same emblem with the same religious significance.
