# Abri: marbled effects on paper

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Abri refers to marbled paper effects discussed in Deccani painting. In the ascetic image, fluid patterned zones interact with the figures and ground. The museum catalogue supplies a historical technique context. The slide alone cannot reconstruct the exact workshop recipe.

## Slide Context

**M3:13, M3:14**

Course reading: The marbling demonstration is placed with Ascetic Riding a Nag, followed by another thin rider on a marbled horse. Process and finished imagery explain how abri patterns become part of a figure-ground composition. The technique comparison is firmer than assigning one exclusive spiritual or literary identity to each rider.

**M3:25**

Course reading: Farrukh Beg’s presumed self-portrait, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Sultan Ali Adil Shah I recap the module through travelling artists, marbled technique and courtly identity. Each supplies a different mechanism of connection. Their grouping makes movement of people, processes and imagery jointly significant.

**M8:20**

Course reading: The Vijaya Vitthala warrior, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Père Tanguy form the final recap. Stone carving, a marbled painting and a European portrait surrounded by Japanese prints supply three different histories of connection. Reclaiming oceanic ties means attending to those mechanisms of encounter and agency rather than treating regions as visually isolated.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The horse’s marbled pattern is ordinary landscape shading painted around an unchanged figure.”

**Why it is wrong:** Abri supplies fluid marbled patterns. Compare the patterned ground around Ascetic Riding a Nag with the adjacent horse whose own body carries marbling: the process changes the relation of figure to ground.
