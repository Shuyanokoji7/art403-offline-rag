# Halos, angels and adapted courtly imagery

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Shah Jahan portrait catalogue documents European angel imagery within an imperial Mughal composition. Such figures can be adapted to glorify a ruler. A halo or winged attendant therefore needs local pictorial context rather than an automatic reading as Christian worship.

## Slide Context

**M3:9**

Course reading: Ibrahim Adil Shah on Atash Khan is paired with enthroned Sarasvati under cultural amalgamation. Courtly self-presentation and a deity of learning and music share the discussion. The next Kitab-e-Nauras page and Sangeet Mahal translate this relationship into text, patronage and a musical setting.

**M3:11**

Course reading: The tambur portrait appears beneath two European prints under A travelling painting. Their arrangement invites comparison of pose, musical subject and travelling images. The European prints, painted portrait and represented instrument have different material roles; proximity alone does not name an exact copied source.

**M3:23**

Course reading: The Golconda Adoration of the Christ Child and Badshahi Ashurkhana interior share the heading Amalgamation of Persianate and European Themes. Painting and ritual architecture demonstrate different forms of religious and visual practice in the same regional discussion. They are complementary examples, not evidence that both serve one ritual.

**M5:10**

Course reading: The Mughal Deposition follows the church and European painting sequence under Derivative? Altered? Creative? The familiar Christian subject becomes a test of adaptation in figures, setting and handling. Slide 17 returns to it beside Raimondi’s print so that a broad encounter can be examined as a particular composition comparison.

**M5:11**

Course reading: The Christian-imagery canteen and Birth of the Prophet Muhammad are grouped under a discussion of early modern empires and Christianity. Their different media and earlier dates widen the history of Abrahamic imagery beyond one Mughal moment. A thematic heading is not the manufacture date of either object.

**M5:17**

Course reading: The Mughal Deposition is placed beside Raimondi’s Descent from the Cross after Raphael. Their shared narrative and related figure organization support a derivative/adaptive comparison. The paired view makes alteration and new handling part of the answer, rather than equating adaptation with mechanical reproduction.

**M5:19, M5:20**

Course reading: The full Jahangir–Malik Ambar allegory and its details connect the emperor’s body to a mapped globe, cosmic supports and a defeated enemy. The India-centred globe is part of a political claim. Repeated enlargement trains attention to the small devices through which cartographic form becomes imperial imagery.

**M5:21, M5:22**

Course reading: Jahangir’s embrace of Shah Abbas uses a mapped globe and unequal figure scale for an allegory of sovereignty; the next folio places Jahangir with Jesus in separate images. The sequence compares the court’s political and religious visual resources. An embrace or shared page does not establish equal power or a single religious identity.

**M5:23, M5:24**

Course reading: Shah Jahan’s halo, angels and portrait pendant are followed by a comparison with the standing-ruler drawing associated in the lecture with Rembrandt. The sequence tracks courtly self-representation and its reception across media. Maker, represented ruler and later interpreter perform different roles in that connection.

**M6:21**

Course reading: Kali/Tara, the Calcutta Art Studio Buddha and the Pune Mahavir print form a sequence of studio devotional images. Different religious subjects use reproducible colour imagery and commercial publication. The significant distinction is between the regional subject, the technology and the agency of the producing studio.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Angels and a halo around Shah Jahan establish him as a Christian ruler.”

**Why it is wrong:** Christian-derived visual devices do not establish that the represented ruler or patron adopted Christianity.
