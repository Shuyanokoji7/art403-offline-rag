# Muraqqa: the assembled album

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: An album can gather paintings and calligraphic specimens with borders and mounts. The resulting page may bring together works of different dates and origins. The course’s composite Gulshan page makes this material assembly explicit. Its components should not all inherit one maker or date.

## Slide Context

**M3:8**

Course reading: The turbaned miniature detail introduces close attention to painted faces and clothing; the next slide locates Farrukh Beg within a travelling artist’s career and album culture. Stylistic observation and a named biography contribute different evidence to the discussion of moving artists.

**M3:11**

Course reading: The tambur portrait appears beneath two European prints under A travelling painting. Their arrangement invites comparison of pose, musical subject and travelling images. The European prints, painted portrait and represented instrument have different material roles; proximity alone does not name an exact copied source.

**M3:12**

Course reading: The Yogini with a Mynah is read through its bird, dress, jewellery, plants and the heading’s question about Andal. It follows musical courtly imagery and precedes the ascetic-and-nag sequence. The grouping probes how courtliness and ascetic identity coexist without settling the figure as one named person.

**M3:23**

Course reading: The Golconda Adoration of the Christ Child and Badshahi Ashurkhana interior share the heading Amalgamation of Persianate and European Themes. Painting and ritual architecture demonstrate different forms of religious and visual practice in the same regional discussion. They are complementary examples, not evidence that both serve one ritual.

**M3:25**

Course reading: Farrukh Beg’s presumed self-portrait, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Sultan Ali Adil Shah I recap the module through travelling artists, marbled technique and courtly identity. Each supplies a different mechanism of connection. Their grouping makes movement of people, processes and imagery jointly significant.

**M5:5, M5:6**

Course reading: Basawan’s Virgin and Child is paired across adjacent slides with a work after Luini. The shared mother-and-child subject supports comparison of gesture, setting and modelling across Mughal and European images. The later date of the displayed Luini copy prevents treating that particular object as Basawan’s direct model.

**M5:12**

Course reading: Ghulam’s Virgin and Child sits within the discussion of collecting and studying classical texts from diverse traditions at the Mughal court. The image becomes a case of active selection and translation between pictorial worlds. The following Bihzad–Abd al-Samad comparison makes artistic lineage a more specific mechanism.

**M5:16**

Course reading: The queried Dürer Crucifixion, Abu’l Hasan’s Saint John and Abdullah Khan’s hawking scene place European print study alongside Mughal portraiture and courtly activity. These examples belong to the lineage discussion while differing in subject and making process. A European religious figure supplies a visual resource, not the identity of every local patron.

**M5:18**

Course reading: The Gulshan sheet assembles a Luna engraving with Persian drawings of a seated man, a group of men and a lion. The whole and its components must be read together: collecting, cutting, mounting and framing create a new relationship among previously separate images. One page therefore need not have one maker, date or original medium.

**M5:22**

Course reading: Jahangir’s embrace of Shah Abbas uses a mapped globe and unequal figure scale for an allegory of sovereignty; the next folio places Jahangir with Jesus in separate images. The sequence compares the court’s political and religious visual resources. An embrace or shared page does not establish equal power or a single religious identity.

**M5:23, M5:24**

Course reading: Shah Jahan’s halo, angels and portrait pendant are followed by a comparison with the standing-ruler drawing associated in the lecture with Rembrandt. The sequence tracks courtly self-representation and its reception across media. Maker, represented ruler and later interpreter perform different roles in that connection.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A muraqqa page is necessarily one continuously illustrated narrative by one maker.”

**Why it is wrong:** An album page is not necessarily a continuously illustrated manuscript narrative or a single artist’s unified painting.
