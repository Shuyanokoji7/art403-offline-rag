# Narasimha, Vasantotsava and the limits of a lecture question

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Module 1 captions bring Narasimha, Chenchu Lakshmi, water and spring festival questions into the reading of the royal complex. The retrieved descriptions establish Mahanavami processions but do not settle the proposed connection for these particular panels and tank. Preserve the question as a question.

## Slide Context

**M1:9, M1:10, M1:11**

Course reading: The location map, three-tiered platform and close views of carved bands develop one argument about the Mahanabami Dibba. Royal ceremony is materialized through spatial elevation and repeated scenes of animals and people. Narasimha and Chenchu Lakshmi keep religious imagery within that programme. The next tank and Rama Temple comparison asks how water, festival and monumental narrative relate.

**M1:12**

Course reading: The stepped tank is paired with the Rama Temple relief of festival and warfare. The juxtaposition asks how water architecture, movement and ritual imagery might relate to Narasimha and Vasantotsava. This is the lecture’s posed connection, while the preceding royal platform supplies the political and ceremonial setting.

**M1:15**

Course reading: The early granite carving and later green-chlorite facing are placed together as phases of the Mahanabami Dibba. Differences in surface, cutting and figural handling are read historically within one monument. The preceding memorial comparison supplies a stylistic reference; the following horse-trade slide shifts attention to the people and goods represented.

**M1:25**

Course reading: The royal platform, horse trader, hero stone and warrior recap the module’s cultural/trade argument. Ceremony, commerce, commemoration and martial imagery contribute different evidence. Their grouping joins social and political functions to travelling forms; it does not reduce Hampi’s art to one exclusively religious or decorative meaning.
