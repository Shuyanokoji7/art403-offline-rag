# Yali and composite animal forms

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Museum accounts describe yali forms through combinations of lion, elephant or horse-like features and their use in southern architecture. Individual specimens vary. The corpus names visible components before applying the composite label, especially when a trunk, open jaws and smaller figures interlock.

## Slide Context

**M1:7**

Course reading: The Krishna Temple hall follows the Hampi maps and precedes the Badami–Vijayanagara sculpture comparison. Its carved posts, horizontal beams and animal supports connect the scale of a building to the sculptural forms through which the next slide compares periods.

**M1:21, M1:22**

Course reading: Vijaya Vitthala friezes, animal-bearing columns and three warrior panels extend the enquiry into interregional imagery beyond the royal platform. Compare clothing, weapons, pose and composite animals within their architectural positions. Differences among the three warriors matter: they are separate carved figures, not duplicate views of one person.

**M1:25**

Course reading: The royal platform, horse trader, hero stone and warrior recap the module’s cultural/trade argument. Ceremony, commerce, commemoration and martial imagery contribute different evidence. Their grouping joins social and political functions to travelling forms; it does not reduce Hampi’s art to one exclusively religious or decorative meaning.

**M3:18, M3:19**

Course reading: The zoomorphic dagger hilt is followed by Sultan Ali Adil Shah I under a question about their relationship. A portable court object and a ruler’s portrait link animal ornament, costume and elite identity. The juxtaposition asks how object culture and represented kingship illuminate one another.

**M3:25**

Course reading: Farrukh Beg’s presumed self-portrait, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Sultan Ali Adil Shah I recap the module through travelling artists, marbled technique and courtly identity. Each supplies a different mechanism of connection. Their grouping makes movement of people, processes and imagery jointly significant.

**M8:20**

Course reading: The Vijaya Vitthala warrior, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Père Tanguy form the final recap. Stone carving, a marbled painting and a European portrait surrounded by Japanese prints supply three different histories of connection. Reclaiming oceanic ties means attending to those mechanisms of encounter and agency rather than treating regions as visually isolated.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A yali is an ordinary zoological species represented identically in every work.”

**Why it is wrong:** A yali is not a natural zoological species, and every composite creature is not necessarily the same mythological being.
