# Costume as evidence, with limits

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Clothing can communicate rank, ritual role and participation in wider courtly fashions. The Vijayanagara dress studies provide a documented example of adopted forms. For an unnamed figure, a costume resemblance remains an observation unless a caption or specific historical source supports a stronger identity claim.

## Slide Context

**M5:4**

Course reading: Akbar and Adham Khan is used to discuss sartorial choices and a ruler’s legitimacy after the muslin sequence. Clothing operates as a political visual language, not simply surface pattern. The following Christian-subject paintings extend the enquiry into the Mughal court’s selective use of different visual resources.

**M7:10**

Course reading: La Japonaise, Cassatt’s The Letter and Mizuno’s After the Bath compare costume performance, domestic pictorial design and a Japanese print. The three illustrate different forms of Japanese reception. Cassatt’s intaglio medium remains distinct from woodblock, and Mizuno’s later print cannot be her direct source.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A person’s hat and robe alone identify nationality and religious allegiance.”

**Why it is wrong:** Do not infer nationality, ethnicity or belief directly from a hat, collar or robe.
