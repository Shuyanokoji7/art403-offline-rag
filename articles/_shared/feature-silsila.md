# Silsila: spiritual transmission

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: A silsila is a lineage of spiritual transmission through Sufi masters. The course uses chains of relation to interpret portraits and gatherings. Distinguish that historical or spiritual connection from a claim that all figures were physically present together when the image was made.

## Slide Context

**M3:20**

Course reading: House of Bijapur brings rulers together in one dynastic composition under Amalgamation of Cultures and Styles. The preceding individual portrait supplies a comparison of scale and political address. A constructed dynastic gathering communicates lineage rather than requiring a single historical sitting.

**M5:13**

Course reading: The two Camel Fight images by or associated with Bihzad and Abd al-Samad are paired under Lineage or Silsila. A recurring subject and composition make transmission and emulation visible. The succeeding European-source studies extend this enquiry beyond one Persianate line of artistic association.

**M5:18**

Course reading: The Gulshan sheet assembles a Luna engraving with Persian drawings of a seated man, a group of men and a lion. The whole and its components must be read together: collecting, cutting, mounting and framing create a new relationship among previously separate images. One page therefore need not have one maker, date or original medium.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A painted spiritual lineage records a single physical meeting of its figures.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: A painted lineage is not automatically documentary group portraiture or proof of a single meeting.
