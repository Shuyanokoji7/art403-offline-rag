# Kalamkari: painted and dyed cotton

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Kalamkari combines drawing with dye processes on cloth. The corpus uses the term for captioned Coromandel and Golconda textiles. Hand drawing, block work and resist stages must be identified separately when evidence permits. Making an image on cotton is not the same operation as painting an opaque miniature on paper.

**M2:14**

Course reading: A coverlet detail and the north doorway of Hayat Baksh’s tomb are paired under Pineapple: a fruit of the trade networks. **M2:15**

Course reading: The Coromandel dodot moves the discussion from motif comparison to use in Southeast Asia. Its pairing in the sequence with European chintz garments on slide 16 shows a production region serving different dress forms and markets. Manufacture and destination are separate roles in the argument.

**M2:16**

Course reading: The chintz jacket and petticoat form a coordinated European dress ensemble. Following the Javanese dodot, they show Indian cotton entering distinct overseas clothing practices. Shared textile circulation therefore produces different social uses and silhouettes, rather than one uniform export style.

**M6:3**

Course reading: The unfinished Pedana woodblocks and finished Polavaram cotton connect a carved matrix to textile printing. This precedes the European Saint Sebastian block-and-impression pair. The sequence compares a printing principle across supports and uses, while keeping the specific practices distinct.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Kalamkari is the name of a floral motif rather than a textile-making practice.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: Kalamkari concerns making images on cloth through drawing and dye processes, with block and resist operations distinguished in the demonstrations. Flowers are one possible motif; the narrative textiles also depict people, architecture and religious stories.
