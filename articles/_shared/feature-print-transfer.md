# Prints as mobile visual resources

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Prints can travel as objects and supply designs for paintings, album pages or new prints. The corpus distinguishes a demonstrable source–adaptation relation from a parallel chosen for comparison. Chronology matters: an object made later cannot have been the direct model for an earlier work.

## Slide Context

**M5:15**

Course reading: The queried Dürer Crucifixion, Abu’l Hasan’s Saint John and Abdullah Khan’s hawking scene place European print study alongside Mughal portraiture and courtly activity. These examples belong to the lineage discussion while differing in subject and making process. A European religious figure supplies a visual resource, not the identity of every local patron.

**M5:17**

Course reading: The Mughal Deposition is placed beside Raimondi’s Descent from the Cross after Raphael. Their shared narrative and related figure organization support a derivative/adaptive comparison. The paired view makes alteration and new handling part of the answer, rather than equating adaptation with mechanical reproduction.

**M5:18**

Course reading: The Gulshan sheet assembles a Luna engraving with Persian drawings of a seated man, a group of men and a lion. The whole and its components must be read together: collecting, cutting, mounting and framing create a new relationship among previously separate images. One page therefore need not have one maker, date or original medium.

**M5:24**

Course reading: Shah Jahan’s halo, angels and portrait pendant are followed by a comparison with the standing-ruler drawing associated in the lecture with Rembrandt. The sequence tracks courtly self-representation and its reception across media. Maker, represented ruler and later interpreter perform different roles in that connection.

**M6:4**

Course reading: The Saint Sebastian woodblock and its impression are paired to make relief printing legible as a transfer process. Raised and cut-away areas relate to printed and unprinted areas. The following lithographic demonstration shows a different printing principle, so all reproducible images cannot be grouped as woodcuts.

**M7:5**

Course reading: Père Tanguy’s Japanese-print backdrop is followed by Sunflowers and Almond Blossoms under Van Gogh’s imagined Japan. Collecting, colour, contour and cropped plant forms offer different ways to engage Japanese imagery. Japanese influence is therefore broader than a Japanese subject or the direct copy of one print.

**M7:7**

Course reading: Zola’s portrait and the enlarged reproductions on its wall make artistic association visible inside an interior. Olympia, a Japanese figure and a monochrome group are objects of pictorial reference within Manet’s painting. The components connect collecting to portrait identity without becoming separately inspected original prints.

**M7:10**

Course reading: La Japonaise, Cassatt’s The Letter and Mizuno’s After the Bath compare costume performance, domestic pictorial design and a Japanese print. The three illustrate different forms of Japanese reception. Cassatt’s intaglio medium remains distinct from woodblock, and Mizuno’s later print cannot be her direct source.

**M8:20**

Course reading: The Vijaya Vitthala warrior, Ascetic Riding a Nag and Père Tanguy form the final recap. Stone carving, a marbled painting and a European portrait surrounded by Japanese prints supply three different histories of connection. Reclaiming oceanic ties means attending to those mechanisms of encounter and agency rather than treating regions as visually isolated.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A later Japanese comparison can be the direct model for an earlier Cassatt print.”

**Why it is wrong:** Japanese-inspired composition need not involve Japanese printing technology; a later comparison is not an earlier source.
