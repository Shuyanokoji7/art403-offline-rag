# VOC and the distinction between collector and maker

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: European companies entered established textile networks.

## Attribution Note

Not established: object provenance; individual maker credit.

## Slide Context

**M4:1, M4:2, M4:3**

Course reading: Vosmaer’s flowers and Vermeer’s Geographer place an oceanic argument within a vase, textile, map and globe. The still life and inhabited interior connect collected goods with geographical knowledge and pictorial skill. The subsequent VOC material makes the commercial network explicit.

**M4:4, M4:5**

Course reading: The VOC ship model and lavish lobster-and-fruit still life connect transport with the consumption and display of goods. Between Amsterdam and Asia develops into a discussion of materiality and trade. The painted table is therefore read through economic circulation as well as arrangement, colour and texture.

**M4:6**

Course reading: The goldsmith’s balance, goods, customers and convex mirror follow the discussion of traded possessions. The shop makes exchange and skilled work visible within a densely organized interior. The next timepiece still life extends attention to crafted objects, time and the values attached to possession.

**M4:7, M4:8**

Course reading: The timepiece and armour still lifes group crafted objects with carefully rendered surfaces. Under the time devoted to making objects discussion, they connect labour and visual attention to possession, transience or status. Different selected objects support different meanings within the still-life format.

**M4:9, M4:10**

Course reading: Peacocks in a garden and dead partridges in a still life compare living display with arranged game. The trade-goods and exotic discussion asks how birds enter collections, consumption and images. Their shared animal subject does not make the scenes interchangeable or give them one fixed symbolic code.

**M8:4, M8:5**

Course reading: Asia in Amsterdam places imported Asian goods near paintings that represent luxury consumption, with vessels and furniture in cases and textiles or pictures on walls. The entrance image and successive gallery views make trade visible through juxtaposition. A painting of a vessel and an exhibited vessel have complementary evidential roles.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A textile in a Dutch collection must have been manufactured by the VOC.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: European collection history and Asian manufacture are compatible; neither should replace the other in an object record.
