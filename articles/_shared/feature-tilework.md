# Tilework as architectural surface

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Glazed tilework can organize colour, pattern and inscription on an architectural surface, as the documented mihrab example demonstrates. Motif, ceramic fabrication and building function should be described separately.

## Slide Context

**M3:3, M3:4**

Course reading: Mahmud Gawan Madrasa is compared with Ulugh Beg’s madrasa and the Registan under Timurid Central Asian culture in Deccan. Monumental entrances, towers and patterned surfaces supply the architectural comparison. The next Bidar landscape slide broadens the enquiry to water, gardens and the siting of buildings.

**M3:23**

Course reading: The Golconda Adoration of the Christ Child and Badshahi Ashurkhana interior share the heading Amalgamation of Persianate and European Themes. Painting and ritual architecture demonstrate different forms of religious and visual practice in the same regional discussion. They are complementary examples, not evidence that both serve one ritual.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A blue-tiled facade and a blue textile must come from the same production centre.”

**Why it is wrong:** A tiled facade is not one painted ceramic slab, and a shared blue colour does not prove a common production centre.
