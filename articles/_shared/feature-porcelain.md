# Porcelain and cross-medium comparison

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Chinese ceramics formed part of long-distance trade. The Belitung cargo provides a specific early maritime case. A ceramic vessel, a painted depiction of it, and a floral pattern compared with porcelain are three different evidence types.

## Slide Context

**M3:9**

Course reading: Ibrahim Adil Shah on Atash Khan is paired with enthroned Sarasvati under cultural amalgamation. Courtly self-presentation and a deity of learning and music share the discussion. The next Kitab-e-Nauras page and Sangeet Mahal translate this relationship into text, patronage and a musical setting.

**M3:12**

Course reading: The Yogini with a Mynah is read through its bird, dress, jewellery, plants and the heading’s question about Andal. It follows musical courtly imagery and precedes the ascetic-and-nag sequence. The grouping probes how courtliness and ascetic identity coexist without settling the figure as one named person.

**M4:5**

Course reading: The VOC ship model and lavish lobster-and-fruit still life connect transport with the consumption and display of goods. Between Amsterdam and Asia develops into a discussion of materiality and trade. The painted table is therefore read through economic circulation as well as arrangement, colour and texture.

**M8:4, M8:5**

Course reading: Asia in Amsterdam places imported Asian goods near paintings that represent luxury consumption, with vessels and furniture in cases and textiles or pictures on walls. The entrance image and successive gallery views make trade visible through juxtaposition. A painting of a vessel and an exhibited vessel have complementary evidential roles.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “A white ground and blue decoration prove Chinese porcelain and a specific trade route.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: White ground and blue decoration alone do not establish porcelain, Chinese manufacture or a particular trade route.
