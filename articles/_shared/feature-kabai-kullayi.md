# Kabai and kullayi: courtly dress

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: Wagoner’s scholarship and the silk-history study discuss the long tunic and conical cap as Islamicate forms adopted within Vijayanagara courtly presentation. The Lepakshi example connects clothing to political self-fashioning and craft exchange. Dress form and personal religious identity remain different questions.

## Slide Context

**M1:20**

Course reading: The three Tirumala bronzes of Krishnadevaraya and his queens are grouped with the Lepakshi mural of kabai and kullayi dress. Courtly clothing and devotional posture connect royal identity to sacred representation across bronze and paint. The preceding procession and following temple carvings locate the ruler within a broader visual programme.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The adoption of kabai and kullayi proves a Vijayanagara ruler’s conversion to Islam.”

**Why it is wrong:** Adopting a garment associated with another courtly culture does not prove the wearer’s conversion or foreign birth.
