# Kali and Tara in the Calcutta diptych

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Met identifies the paired print as Kali and Tara, wrathful protectors in a Mahavidya context. Their appearance, supporting figures and surroundings differ within the same sheet. Preserve the left/right distinction and the specific visual attributes rather than treating both halves as duplicated images of one goddess.

## Slide Context

**M6:19**

Course reading: Kali/Tara, the Calcutta Art Studio Buddha and the Pune Mahavir print form a sequence of studio devotional images. Different religious subjects use reproducible colour imagery and commercial publication. The significant distinction is between the regional subject, the technology and the agency of the producing studio.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “Kali and Tara are two identical impressions of one goddess on the same sheet.”

**Why it is wrong:** Kali and Tara occupy separate halves of one diptych. Compare their held objects, crowns, garlands and garments: related fierce imagery is organized into two distinct goddess subjects.
