# Balances: weighing, value and allegory

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The Goldsmith painting contains a working balance in a shop setting. The lecture also reads a balance within Jahangir’s allegorical apparatus. The same recognizable object can serve practical measurement in one image and a claim about justice in another. The latter is the course’s interpretation.

## Slide Context

**M4:6**

Course reading: The goldsmith’s balance, goods, customers and convex mirror follow the discussion of traded possessions. The shop makes exchange and skilled work visible within a densely organized interior. The next timepiece still life extends attention to crafted objects, time and the values attached to possession.

**M5:19, M5:20**

Course reading: The full Jahangir–Malik Ambar allegory and its details connect the emperor’s body to a mapped globe, cosmic supports and a defeated enemy. The India-centred globe is part of a political claim. Repeated enlargement trains attention to the small devices through which cartographic form becomes imperial imagery.

## Distinctions

**Exam distractor:** “The goldsmith’s balance and Jahangir’s allegorical balance identify the same occupation.”

**Why it is wrong:** Course reading: The goldsmith’s balance belongs to weighing and exchange in a shop. Jahangir’s balance belongs to the allegorical assertion of justice and sovereignty.
