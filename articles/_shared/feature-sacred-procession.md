# Procession, ceremony and organized movement

## Definition and Grounded Significance

Course reading: The documented Mahanavami setting includes royal viewing of processions. Relief bands represent horses, soldiers and courtly life. In each linked image, identify moving figures, attendants and the depicted focus before deciding whether the procession is royal, devotional or both.

## Slide Context

**M1:19**

Course reading: The Virupaksha procession mural is placed beside its temple gateway under Images of procession cross Media. Painted movement and monumental setting are read together, continuing the earlier relief and textile comparisons. The following royal bronzes and Lepakshi mural extend the question from processions to the representation of rulers.

**M1:21**

Course reading: Vijaya Vitthala friezes, animal-bearing columns and three warrior panels extend the enquiry into interregional imagery beyond the royal platform. Compare clothing, weapons, pose and composite animals within their architectural positions. Differences among the three warriors matter: they are separate carved figures, not duplicate views of one person.

**M2:8**

Course reading: The Lepakshi procession mural and Golconda hanging compare figures, costume and ordered movement across wall painting and cloth. They follow the bird comparison across textile, jewellery and stone. The sequence makes visual translation between media part of the textile’s cultural history.

**M2:13**

Course reading: The Golconda coverlet is paired with the hanging of Manikkavachar and Meenakshi’s wedding. Courtly imagery and religious narrative distinguish the two textiles even where borders and repeated figures invite formal comparison. The heading asks how to differentiate kalamkari works, not how to treat them as one subject.
