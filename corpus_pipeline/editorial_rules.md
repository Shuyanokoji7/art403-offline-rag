# Corpus authoring contract — confidence and slide context

These rules apply to every new or revised entity and override the original fixed-field completion policy.

1. Tier 1 is direct visual observation. State shape, colour, composition, placement and visible contents plainly. Do not hedge an observed flower because its maker is unknown.
2. Tier 2 is a reading supported by lecture headings, notes, grouping or argument. Prefix every such interpretive passage with `Course reading:`. Preserve a question as a question when the lecture itself poses one.
3. Tier 3 is an unsupported external attribution or fact: exact date, named maker, confirmed origin, specific material identification or a contested identity. Consolidate these into one attribution note per article. The phrase `not established` may appear there once; never fill individual sections with null sentences.
4. The type brief is a menu of applicable questions, not a form whose empty fields require prose. Omit any section without substantive content. Inapplicable figural fields do not belong on a nonfigurative decorative object.
5. Before analysis, gather all entities on every shared slide and all entities in the same heading group, including its continuation on adjacent slides. Keep exact row identities and links. Write a `Slide Context` argument explaining what the juxtaposition does; a list of neighbours is insufficient. Do not infer a repeated motif where the neighbour contributes a different but complementary role.
6. Use `decorative_object` for standalone ceramic, ivory, glass, lacquer or enamel objects. Keep a painting of a vase as a painting and a gallery arrangement of ceramics as an exhibition display.
7. End with one concrete exam distractor and its specific correction. Suitable errors confuse subject with technique, omit a named significance dimension, confuse maker/patron/collector roles, or replace a specific display argument with a vague or false one. Add labelled comparison relationships where relevant; do not replace the distractor with a generic disclaimer.
8. Keep raw lecture evidence separate from analysis and retain explicit catalogue corrections. Confidence applies to claims, not to whether an entire article sounds cautious.
